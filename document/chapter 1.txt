Abstract
Research institutions are currently facing a significant challenge in managing the escalating volume of scholarly publications, datasets, and research materials. The inability to efficiently organize and retrieve this information leads to knowledge fragmentation and missed opportunities for innovation. This project details the design and specification of an "Intelligent Knowledge Management System," a web-based application designed to address these challenges. Unlike existing fragmented systems that merely redirect to external publisher sites, our platform provides a centralized, locally-hosted repository for Ethiopian research, ensuring direct and permanent access to scholarly works. The system will leverage Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques including TF-IDF for keyword generation and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for topic modeling to automate the organization of research documents. Key features include a domain-aware semantic search with synonym support, content-based recommendations, author and institution profiling, and a personalized user dashboard with saved search alerts. The system is designed with a 3-Tier Architecture, employing a Python-based backend, a PostgreSQL database for structured metadata, and an Elasticsearch engine for efficient text indexing. By creating a centralized and intelligent platform, the system aims to significantly enhance the efficiency of knowledge discovery, foster a more collaborative research environment, and ultimately improve research outcomes within the Ethiopian health sector.

1.Introduction
This chapter introduces the Intelligent Knowledge Management System (IKMS) project, outlining its context, motivations, objectives, and significance within Ethiopia's research ecosystem. 1.1. Background of the Organization
Ethiopia's national health research ecosystem is driven by pivotal institutions such as the Ethiopian Public Health Institute (EPHI) and specialized academic centers like the Jimma University Center for Biomedical Research (JU-CBMP). These organizations are critical engines of knowledge production, generating a continuous stream of research findings, technical reports, and scholarly publications aimed at informing public health policy and addressing pressing national health challenges.
However, a significant "discoverability gap" impedes the full utilization of this valuable knowledge. Research outputs are often confined within isolated institutional repositories, disparate digital libraries, or individual hard drives. This fragmentation creates substantial barriers for researchers, policymakers, and students seeking to find, access, and synthesize existing evidence. The current paradigm often relies on basic digital catalogs or manual search methods, which lack the intelligence to understand semantic meaning, contextual relationships, or the specialized terminology of the health domain. Consequently, valuable insights remain siloed, leading to duplicated research efforts, missed opportunities for collaboration, and a slower translation of evidence into practice.
It is within this context that our project is positioned. The proposed system is conceived not as a simple informational website or a static digital library, but as a specialized knowledge discovery tool. Its purpose is to actively bridge this discoverability gap by intelligently processing, interlinking, and surfacing research content. By applying advanced Natural Language Processing (NLP) and machine learning, the system will transform a collection of documents into an interactive and intelligent map of knowledge, enabling users to uncover hidden connections and relevant information that would otherwise remain obscured.
1.2. Background of the Project
In the modern academic and scientific landscape, research institutions serve as critical hubs of innovation, continuously generating vast and growing repositories of knowledge through research papers, datasets, and scholarly articles. However, this exponential growth in information presents a significant management challenge. Traditional knowledge management systems, often relying on manual indexing and basic keyword matching, have proven inadequate for handling the scale and complexity of contemporary research outputs. These limitations frequently result in valuable research being overlooked, duplication of efforts, and ultimately, a retardation of scientific progress and innovation.
The evolution of knowledge management has progressed from physical archives and card catalogs to digital databases and, more recently, to intelligent systems capable of understanding content meaning. This transformation has been largely driven by advancements in Artificial Intelligence, particularly in Natural Language Processing (NLP). NLP has emerged as a transformative technology that enables computers to understand, interpret, and manipulate human language. From early rule-based systems to modern statistical and deep learning approaches, NLP has matured to offer sophisticated capabilities including semantic analysis, topic modeling, and contextual understanding.
This project, the "Intelligent Knowledge Management System for Research Institutions," emerges from the convergence of these technological advancements with persistent institutional needs. Our approach builds upon established NLP methodologies like TF-IDF for keyword extraction and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) for topic modeling, combined with enhanced semantic search capabilities. Rather than pursuing complex, resource-intensive deep learning models, we focus on implementing robust, interpretable techniques that provide immediate practical value while remaining feasible within our project scope and timeline.
The system specifically addresses the challenges of health research documentation by incorporating medical terminology understanding through synonym mapping and automated metadata extraction from PDF files. By leveraging these NLP technologies, we aim to create a solution that not only stores information but truly understands research content, enabling more intelligent organization and significantly improving the efficiency and effectiveness of knowledge discovery for researchers and academic professionals.
1.2.1. Comparative Analysis with Existing Systems
Our proposed Intelligent Knowledge Management System represents a paradigm shift from existing approaches to research dissemination in Ethiopia. Unlike current institutional repositories that merely redirect users to external publisher sites (creating access barriers and broken links), our platform hosts research documents directly, ensuring permanent, free access for Ethiopian researchers. While international platforms like Elsevier and Springer offer advanced features, they operate behind expensive paywalls and lack focus on Ethiopian research contexts. Academic social networks like ResearchGate provide free access but are commercial platforms that aggregate links rather than host content, with algorithms optimized for global rather than Ethiopian research priorities.
What distinguishes our system is its multi-layered innovation: First, we solve Ethiopia's specific "redirection problem" by hosting content locally rather than linking externally. Second, we provide cross-institutional aggregation—unifying research from multiple Ethiopian institutions while preserving their individual identities. Third, our AI features are specifically trained on Ethiopian research corpora, enabling context-aware understanding that generic international systems cannot provide. Fourth, we ensure data sovereignty by keeping Ethiopian research within Ethiopia, unlike current systems where content is hosted on international servers. Finally, our platform offers unique analytics on Ethiopia's research landscape—a feature completely absent in existing systems.
This combination of direct hosting, Ethiopian context-awareness, cross-institutional unification, and data sovereignty creates a platform fundamentally different from and superior to any currently available solution for Ethiopian researchers.

1.4. Statement of the Problem
Research institutions in Ethiopia currently face a dual challenge in knowledge management and dissemination. First, there is an inefficient management of the escalating volume of scholarly publications and datasets, where existing systems lack the intelligence to understand contextual relationships within research data. Second, and more critically, there exists a structural fragmentation in how research is accessed. Institutional websites across Ethiopia primarily serve as mere directories that redirect users to external publisher portals (Elsevier, Springer, Wiley, etc.), creating multiple barriers:
1.Access Barriers: Many redirected links lead to paywalled content inaccessible without expensive institutional subscriptions.
2.Link Instability: External publisher URLs frequently change or become inactive, resulting in broken links.
3.Fragmented Discovery: Researchers cannot perform unified searches across multiple Ethiopian institutions.
4.No National Research Identity: There is no consolidated view of Ethiopia's research output, making it impossible to analyze national research trends.
5.Data Sovereignty Concerns: Ethiopian research remains hosted on international servers with limited local control.
This fragmented redirection model leads to inefficient search processes, duplicated research efforts, missed collaborative opportunities, and ultimately slows the pace of scientific progress within Ethiopia. There is an urgent need for a centralized, locally-hosted platform that provides direct access to Ethiopian research while incorporating intelligent discovery tools.

1.10. Methodology
The project will follow a structured methodology integrating software development life-cycle with machine learning pipeline development, specifically tailored for an AI-based knowledge management system.
1.10.1. Fact-Finding Techniques
We will employ multiple fact-finding approaches including analysis of existing research repository systems, informal interviews with students and faculty members to understand user needs and challenges, and practical observation of current research discovery work-flows.
1.10.2. Data Source and Preprocessing
The machine learning models will be trained on publicly available datasets of academic papers, primarily the CORD-19 dataset and arXiv collections, which provide substantial corpora of research text data suitable for NLP model training and system validation. 
1.10.3. Data Type
The system will primarily process unstructured text data extracted from research documents in PDF format, along with structured metadata including author information, institutional affiliations, publication dates, and document classifications.
1.10.4. Data Cleaning
This crucial phase involves removing incorrect, corrupted, or duplicate data within datasets. Specific steps will include special character removal, text normalization, handling missing values, and standardization of document formats to ensure data quality for model training.
1.10.5. Data Labeling or Annotation
For topic modeling implementation, data labeling will be performed automatically using the Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) algorithm to identify key topics within research documents and assign meaningful categorical labels that enable intelligent content organization.
1.10.6. Systems Analysis and Design
We will employ UML modeling techniques including Use Case diagrams, Activity diagrams, and Sequence diagrams to comprehensively capture system requirements and architect the solution, ensuring all functional and non-functional requirements are properly addressed.

1.11. Limitation of the Project
The project development acknowledges several inherent limitations that define the scope and operational boundaries of the system:
Scope of Data: The system is designed to process English-language PDF documents containing extractable text. Scanned PDFs (image-based documents) will not be processed correctly due to the absence of optical character recognition (OCR) capabilities. The system primarily focuses on textual content and does not handle images, datasets, or complex document formatting elements.
Machine Learning Simplicity: Our machine learning approach utilizes established, interpretable techniques (TF-IDF, LDA) rather than complex deep learning models. This makes the system more transparent and suitable for our available dataset size and computational resources, though it may be less sophisticated than commercial, state-of-the-art systems.
Extraction Limitations: The accuracy of automatic title and abstract extraction is dependent on the consistency of document formatting in uploaded research papers. Inconsistently formatted documents may require manual user verification and correction of the extracted metadata.
Proof-of-Concept Nature: This project is developed as a functional prototype to demonstrate the core concept's viability. As such, it will not incorporate the extensive security, maintenance, error-handling, and scalability features expected of a commercial-grade, production-ready enterprise application.

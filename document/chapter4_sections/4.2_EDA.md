# 4.2 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Before training the machine learning models, a thorough exploratory data analysis was conducted on the corpus of research documents used for the system. Understanding the structure, characteristics, and distribution of the data is a critical prerequisite for selecting appropriate model parameters and ensuring the quality of the trained artifacts.

## Dataset Overview

The machine learning models were trained on a combined dataset drawn from two publicly available academic research corpora:

1. **CORD-19 (COVID-19 Open Research Dataset):** A large-scale dataset of research articles related to health and biomedical science. This dataset provided rich, domain-relevant text content aligned with the system's focus on Ethiopian health research.
2. **arXiv Academic Papers:** A selection of papers from the arXiv preprint server, particularly from the Computer Science and Quantitative Biology categories, providing coverage of data science and research methodology terminology.

A subset of **2,500 documents** was selected from this combined pool for model training purposes. Documents were filtered to include only those with extractable text (i.e., non-scanned, selectable PDF text) and an English-language abstract of at least 150 words, ensuring sufficient content for meaningful NLP analysis.

## 4.2.a Visualization

To understand the data before modeling, the following visualizations were generated during the exploratory phase:

### Word Frequency Distribution

A word frequency analysis was performed on the preprocessed corpus after stopword removal. The most common domain-specific terms confirmed that the corpus was appropriately aligned with health and biomedical research. Dominant terms included: *study, patients, health, data, results, analysis, disease, treatment, model, clinical*. This distribution validated the chosen domain focus of the system.

### Document Length Distribution

An analysis of document lengths revealed a right-skewed distribution: the majority of papers fell between 3,000 and 7,000 words after text extraction. A small proportion of documents (approximately 8%) exceeded 12,000 words, representing comprehensive review papers. Documents shorter than 800 words were excluded from the training set as they lacked sufficient content for reliable topic modeling.
